# Using the Error Tracker when planning

The `english-analysis` skill keeps a `## Error Tracker` section in the student's record (`~/eng-teaching-records/<slug>.md`). It lists error types by code, with a rate per 1,000 student words for each analyzed lesson, a status, and the latest quoted examples. Use it to decide what a lesson should work on. If the record has no such section, skip all of this and plan as usual.

## Contents
- Reading the table
- Turning codes into lesson content
- Practice built from error patterns, never from the student's sentences
- Stuck means change the method
- What not to do

## Reading the table
- Rows are ranked by priority (latest rate times impact on understanding). Work from the top.
- Status words: **stuck** (flat for three lessons), **rising**, **new**, **improving**, **fading**, **steady**, **minor**, **baseline**.
- With fewer than three analyzed lessons the table says it is provisional. Treat statuses as hints, not verdicts.
- Rates are relative to this student's earlier lessons only. Never compare students.

## Turning codes into lesson content
Pick grammar points from the top rows that are stuck, rising or new. Respect the tutor's grammar depth setting and any "light grammar" preference in the profile.

| Code | Grammar point or lesson focus | Activities that practice it well |
|---|---|---|
| BE | be with present simple vs. continuous, short answers | Inbox activity, Phone Call Simulation, Role Play |
| SVA | subject-verb agreement, there is/there are, each/every | Sentence upgrades, Speaking Practice with a target structure |
| TENSE | past simple vs. present perfect, narrative tenses, time markers | Story Chain, Voicemail Story, Speaking Practice (retell with a fixed tense) |
| FEEL | -ed vs. -ing adjectives, get/be with feelings | Odd One Out, Sentence upgrades, Role Play |
| ART | articles with first and second mention, a/an vs. the vs. none | Read a generated dialogue, Sentence upgrades |
| PLUR | countable vs. uncountable, much/many, possessive 's | Ranking scenario, Odd One Out |
| VPREP | verb + preposition patterns taught as chunks | Fresh Vocabulary with collocations, Read a generated dialogue |
| COMP | comparatives and superlatives, as...as, the same as | Ranking scenario, Impossible choice, This or That |
| WFORM | word families and suffixes | Fresh Vocabulary with collocations, Odd One Out |
| QFORM | questions and indirect questions | Press conference, Phone Call Simulation, Inbox activity |
| ORDER | word order, adverb placement | Sentence upgrades, Story Chain |
| LINK | linking words for contrast, cause and result | Mini Debates, Decision Meeting |
| WCHOICE | near-synonyms, false friends, register | Odd One Out, Sentence upgrades, Fresh Vocabulary |
| COLLOC | verb + noun collocations | Fresh Vocabulary with collocations, Read a generated dialogue |

Keep it to one new grammar point per lesson (existing rule). When several codes point to the same fix (for example ART and PLUR both fit a noun-phrase lesson), combine them in one focus.

## Practice built from error patterns, never from the student's sentences
Never put the student's own sentences into a lesson, a handout or a worksheet, not even adapted. It feels like being watched. The tracker's quotes and the errors .tsv are for the tutor's eyes only.

- **Review of Prior Content:** build the grammar retrieval task as 3 to 4 new "Fix the mistake" sentences on the lesson's theme and vocabulary that contain the same kind of error, written fresh. Present them as plain sentences. Do not write "your mistakes" or "you said", and do not name the student.
- **Gap-fills and error-correction activities:** take the pattern, not the wording, and write new sentences about the lesson's topic.
- **Prefer recent, high-priority codes.** Skip anything marked fading or minor unless it is a one-line recap.
- If the profile says the tutor wants light grammar or gentle feedback, use recasts and retells instead of correction drills, and keep the fix-the-sentence set to two items.

## Stuck means change the method
A code marked stuck means more of the same has not worked. Do not schedule another drill of the same kind. Choose a different route:
- teach the pattern as a chunk or phrase (not a rule) and use it in a role play;
- have the student retell a story of their own with the target form, then retell it again after feedback on only that form;
- use a noticing task: give a short generated text containing the pattern and ask the student to find and underline it before using it;
- add a Teacher's Note under the Grammar Focus naming the code, how long it has been stuck, and the new method being tried.

## What not to do
- Do not turn every lesson into an error lesson. The theme, interests and goal still lead; the tracker picks the grammar point inside that.
- Do not print the tracker, its codes, its numbers or any of the student's own sentences in student-facing text or in the handout.
- Do not change the learning path rows already delivered. When the tracker disagrees with an upcoming row, tell the tutor what it shows and offer to swap the grammar point; the tutor decides.
